Built Like Someone Who Was Staying

Most homes at this price on the corridor have one septic system. This one has three — one for the kitchen, one for the laundry, one for the bathrooms and hot tub — plus a master overflow if any of them fail. In fifteen-plus years of existence, this house has never had a sewage problem. That’s not luck.

The water system follows the same logic. A 10,000-liter underground tank. Two well pumps with a full backup. Municipal water arrives 24 hours a day, 7 days a week — no trucks, no rationing, no interruptions. Notarios live on this street. Hotels operate nearby. The water doesn’t stop here, and it never has.

The windows and doors were custom-ordered from Germany. Full double-pane. The result: standing inside the living room with the windows closed, you cannot hear the Cabo wind. One visiting agent said it. Every buyer who walks through notices it.

The hurricane shutters came from Florida — the best place in the world to source hurricane protection. Custom-measured, individually fitted to every window and door on the upper level. The house seals completely. Leave for six months. Come back to exactly what you left.

The pool is PebbleTech. Not tile. Tile cracks. Tile falls. PebbleTech doesn’t. It looks the same as the day it was finished.

The outdoor furniture on the upper terrace is Amish-made composite, brought in from Pennsylvania. It has been sitting in the Cabo sun and salt air for five years. It looks new.

The plumbing throughout the house was fully replaced by the current owner when he took possession. Not patched. Replaced.

Nobody does any of this for a spec build. You do it when you’re the one who has to live there.

What Life Looks Like From This Address

Ten minutes to Cabo San Lucas. Ten minutes to San José del Cabo. One mile from Cabo Real Golf Course. Ten-minute walk to Chileno Bay beach — one of the few genuinely swimmable beaches on the corridor.

From November through April, humpback whales pass through. You can watch them from the upper terrace with a pair of binoculars. In June and July, sea turtles nest on the beach nearby. Several nights a week, fireworks go up over the Sea of Cortez and you can see them from the terrace with a cold drink in your hand.

The maid’s quarters has its own exterior entrance and staircase, with a bathroom already roughed in. It functions as a completely independent unit — guest suite, staff quarters, or a short-term rental running separately from the rest of the house.

The community is gated and guarded. Paved roads. A hospital is under construction nearby. Local amenities are expanding. El Tule is growing, and it’s still early.
